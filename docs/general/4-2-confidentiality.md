# 4.2 Confidentiality

## In one sentence

Treat everything a customer gives you or you learn while working for them
as confidential, agree in advance what (if anything) will be made public,
and make every person working for the lab bound by that promise.

## What an assessor looks for

- **A binding commitment**: confidentiality in contracts or terms of
  business, so customers can rely on it.
- **Customers told in advance** about any information the lab plans to make
  public (for example, results in a published list of certified products).
- **A rule for disclosure required by law**: the customer is told, unless
  the law forbids it.
- **Information about a customer from other sources** (a complainant, a
  regulator) also kept confidential, including the source when asked.
- **Everyone covered**: staff, temporary staff, students, committee members,
  contractors and external IT providers, through signed agreements.
- **Practical protection**: access to systems and files, clean desks, safe
  disposal of samples and paper, care with email.

## How to do it in a small lab

1. **Put confidentiality in your terms of business** and quotes.
2. **Have everyone sign an agreement** when they start: staff, students,
   cleaners with access to offices, IT contractors. Keep the signed copies.
3. **Decide what may become public** for each type of work (for example, a
   product listed as tested in a certification scheme) and tell the
   customer at contract review ([7.1](../process/7-1-contract-review.md)).
4. **Protect the information in practice**: individual logins, access by
   role, locked storage for retained samples, shredding, encrypted laptops,
   and checking recipients before sending reports.
5. **Write down the legal exceptions**, such as a court order or a
   regulator's legal request, and who decides how to answer.
6. **Think about samples and photos too.** A photo of a prototype boiler
   on social media is a confidentiality breach.

## Worked example

A made-up biofuel lab receives an email from the national environment
agency. It asks for all emission test results for one boiler maker's
models over the past two years.

| Step | What the lab does |
|---|---|
| Check the basis | The quality manager asks the agency which law gives it the right to the data. The agency points to a market surveillance law that allows it to require test data. |
| Check the contract | The lab's terms say results are given only to the customer unless the law requires otherwise, in which case the customer is told. |
| Tell the customer | The lab writes to the boiler maker, explaining what was requested and on what basis, before sending anything. |
| Send only what is required | Results for the named models only, not the maker's design drawings in the job files. |
| Record | The request, the legal basis, the letter to the customer and what was sent are filed in the customer's record. |

When a competitor phones and asks "Did model X pass at your lab?", the
answer is that the lab cannot discuss any customer's work.

## Common findings

- **No signed confidentiality agreements** for students, temporary staff
  or IT contractors.
- **Customers not told** what information will be made public.
- **Shared logins or open folders** where any staff member can see every
  customer's files.
- **Reports emailed to the wrong person**, with no check before sending.
- **Retained samples or job files** left where visitors can see them.
