# 4.1 Impartiality

## In one sentence

Make sure nothing (money, relationships, pressure from a customer or your
own management) can push your results in one direction, by finding those
risks before they cause harm and dealing with them.

## What an assessor looks for

- **A clear commitment from top management** to impartiality, written
  down and known by staff.
- **An impartiality risk register** (or a similar record) that is real and
  specific to your lab, not a generic list. It should cover risks from
  ownership, finance, customers, suppliers, staff relationships and
  shared resources.
- **Evidence the register is kept up to date**: reviewed at regular
  intervals and when something changes, such as a new owner, a new big
  customer or a new member of staff from a customer's company.
- **Actions for each significant risk**, and evidence they work.
- **Staff who understand it.** Expect a question like "What would you do if
  a customer asked you to re-test until the sample passed?"
- **Declarations** from staff about interests that could affect their work,
  where your lab uses them.

## How to do it in a small lab

1. **Write a short statement** signed by top management: the lab's results
   are not influenced by commercial, financial or other pressure, and staff
   can raise concerns without fear.
2. **List where pressure could come from.** Think about:
    - who owns the lab and what else they sell;
    - customers that bring a large share of income;
    - staff who used to work for a customer, or have family there;
    - bonuses or targets linked to customers' results;
    - consulting or advice given to the same customer you test for;
    - shared equipment, people or premises with another company.
3. **Rate each risk** (simple high, medium, low is enough) and decide what
   removes or reduces it.
4. **Review the register** at least once a year (management review is a
   good moment) and whenever something changes.
5. **Ask staff to declare interests** when they join and when things change.
6. **Make "no" safe.** Staff must know they can refuse pressure and who to
   tell.

## Worked example

A made-up biofuel test lab belongs to a group that also owns a pellet
plant. Part of its impartiality risk register:

| Risk | Rating | Action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| The group's pellet plant is a customer; results affect its sales | High | Plant results authorised by the technical manager, who reports to the institute director, not to the plant; plant staff have no access to the lab's systems | Organisation chart; access list |
| One customer brings 35 % of income | Medium | No staff incentives linked to any customer; income reviewed at management review | Bonus policy; review minutes |
| New analyst moved from a customer's quality department | Medium | Declaration of interest; not assigned to that customer's samples for 12 months | Signed declaration; work allocation |
| A customer asks for "advice to improve their pellets" | Medium | Advice only as general information, recorded; the lab does not design the customer's process | Advice log |
| A salesperson promises "fast pass results" | High | Sales staff trained; standard wording for quotes; complaints monitored | Training record |

At the yearly review, the quality manager adds a new risk: a staff member
has started a part-time role with a boiler maker. A declaration is signed
and that person no longer tests that maker's boilers.

## Common findings

- **A generic register** copied from a template, with no risks specific to
  the lab's owners, customers or staff.
- **Risks identified but no action** or no follow-up.
- **The register never reviewed** after changes such as a new owner or a
  major customer.
- **Staff unaware** of the policy or what to do when pressured.
- **Incentives linked to customers' results or volumes** that nobody
  considered a risk.
