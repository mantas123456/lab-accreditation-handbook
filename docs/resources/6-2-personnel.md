# 6.2 Personnel, competence and authorisation

## In one sentence

Decide what each role needs people to know and be able to do, train and
supervise people until they can do it, authorise them in writing for
specific tasks, and keep checking that they still can.

## What an assessor looks for

- **Competence requirements for each role**: education, qualifications,
  training, technical knowledge, skills and experience, written down.
- **A process** for selecting, training, supervising, authorising and
  monitoring staff, and records showing it was followed for each person.
- **Written authorisation** for specific tasks, especially:
    - developing, changing, verifying and validating methods;
    - analysing results, including statements of conformity and opinions;
    - reviewing, reporting and authorising results.
- **Supervision** of people still in training, so their work is checked.
- **Ongoing monitoring of competence**, not only a one-off training record.
- **Staff who know their duties**, and know the limits of their
  authorisation.

## How to do it in a small lab

1. **Write a short competence profile** for each role: for an analyst, the
   methods, the equipment, the calculations and the QC rules they must
   master.
2. **Train in steps** for each method: read the SOP; watch an authorised
   analyst; do tests under supervision; then show competence on your own.
3. **Set the proof of competence before training starts.** For example:
   duplicates within the method's repeatability limit, a blind control
   sample within the QC limits, and an observed test with no errors.
4. **Authorise per method and task.** Keep an authorisation matrix:
   people down the side, methods and tasks across the top, with dates and
   the authoriser's signature.
5. **Monitor competence every year** with a mix of: observed tests, QC and
   PT results by analyst, report review findings, and a short discussion.
6. **Withdraw or renew** authorisation when someone has not done a test for
   a long time or monitoring shows a problem.

## Worked example

A made-up biofuel lab trains a new analyst, E, on the total moisture test.

| Step | Evidence | Date | Signed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read SOP MO-02 rev. 3 and the uncertainty budget | Reading record | 3 Mar | E |
| Observed C carry out 3 tests | Training log | 4–5 Mar | C |
| 6 tests under supervision; worksheets checked | Worksheets with C's initials | 6–12 Mar | C |
| Duplicates: all 6 pairs within the method's repeatability limit | Data in training file | 12 Mar | C |
| Blind control sample: 8.21 % (QC chart centre 8.19 %, ±2 SD 7.98 to 8.40) | QC record | 13 Mar | C |
| Explained the calculation, buoyancy correction and QC rules | Interview notes | 13 Mar | B |
| **Authorised for total moisture, testing only** | Authorisation matrix | 14 Mar | B (technical manager) |

E is not yet authorised to check other analysts' results or to authorise
reports. Those come after six months of routine testing.

**Monitoring.** A year later, B observes one of E's tests and reviews E's
QC results for the year (no rejects caused by E) and E's PT result
(z = 0.4). The authorisation is confirmed.

## Common findings

- **Training records but no authorisation**, or authorisation with no
  evidence of competence behind it.
- **Authorisation that does not match the work**: people authorising
  reports or giving opinions without being authorised to.
- **No monitoring after authorisation**, sometimes for years.
- **Competence requirements missing** for some roles, often managers and
  support staff.
- **Temporary staff and students** working without supervision records.
