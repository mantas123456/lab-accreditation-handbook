# 6.6 Externally provided products and services

## In one sentence

For anything bought in that affects your results, decide what you need,
choose providers that can deliver it, check what arrives before you use
it, and keep watching how providers perform.

## What an assessor looks for

- **A procedure** covering which products and services are in scope and
  how they are handled.
- **What is in scope**: anything that
    - goes into your lab activities (reagents, reference materials, gases,
      consumables);
    - supports your operation (calibration, maintenance, software, IT);
    - goes to your customer as it is (subcontracted tests).
- **Requirements defined and agreed** with the provider before ordering.
- **Criteria for choosing and monitoring providers**, and records of
  evaluations: an approved list or similar.
- **Checks on arrival** before items or services are used.
- **Action taken** when a provider performs badly.

## How to do it in a small lab

1. **List what matters.** Usually: calibration services, reference
   materials, critical reagents and gases, equipment maintenance, software
   or IT services that hold results, and subcontract labs.
2. **Set criteria per type.**
    - Calibration labs: accredited for the calibration and range you need.
    - Reference materials: from an accredited producer, with a certificate.
    - Subcontract labs: accredited for the test, method agreed, customer
      informed ([7.1](../process/7-1-contract-review.md)).
    - Reagents: grade and purity stated, certificate of analysis.
3. **Keep an approved provider list** with the date and basis of approval.
4. **Specify exactly what you want** on the order: the calibration points
   and range, the certificate content, the grade.
5. **Check on arrival** and record it: certificate content, expiry date,
   damage, the right item.
6. **Re-evaluate once a year**: problems found, late deliveries, failed
   checks, changes in the provider's accreditation.

## Worked example

A made-up biofuel lab's approved provider list, extract:

| Provider | Supplies | Criteria | Last evaluation | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calibration lab A | Balance and weights calibration | Accredited for mass, range up to 220 g | January | Approved; certificates complete, on time |
| Calibration lab B | Thermometer calibration | Accredited for temperature, 0–150 °C | January | **Conditional**: range on last certificate stopped at 100 °C; next order specifies 150 °C |
| RM producer C | Benzoic acid for calorimetry | Accredited reference material producer | January | Approved |
| Lab D (subcontract) | Chlorine in fuel | Accredited for the method; turnaround 10 days | January | Approved; one late result, discussed |
| Gas supplier E | Oxygen for the calorimeter | Purity grade stated on certificate | January | Approved |

**Check on arrival.** When the thermometer comes back from lab B, the
quality manager checks the certificate before T-02 goes back into use:
range now 0–150 °C, results and uncertainty present, accreditation mark
present. The check is recorded and B returns to "Approved".

## Common findings

- **No evaluation records** for key providers, or a list that is never
  reviewed.
- **Accreditation of a calibration lab checked, but not its scope.**
- **Items used before the incoming check**, or no record of it.
- **Subcontracting without the customer's agreement.**
- **IT and software services forgotten** although they hold results.
