# 6.3 Facilities and environmental conditions

## In one sentence

Work in rooms whose conditions do not affect your results, know which
conditions matter for each activity, and monitor, control and record them
where they do.

## What an assessor looks for

- **Documented requirements** for facilities and conditions, where they
  matter: temperature, humidity, dust, vibration, light, electrical supply,
  and so on, taken from the methods and the equipment manuals.
- **Monitoring and records** of the conditions that matter, with action
  when they go outside the limits.
- **Control of the facilities**: who can enter which areas, how
  contamination and interference are prevented, and how incompatible
  activities are kept apart.
- **The same care at other sites**: customer premises, temporary sites and
  mobile labs, when you work there.

## How to do it in a small lab

1. **Read your methods and manuals for conditions.** Note the limits for
   each room and activity. Many are in the equipment manual (the
   calorimeter's room temperature range, the balance's draught and
   vibration needs).
2. **Separate incompatible work.** Keep dusty milling away from balances
   and fine analytical work; keep high-temperature furnaces away from
   temperature-sensitive instruments.
3. **Monitor what matters**, ideally with data loggers that record
   automatically and alarm when a limit is passed. Calibrate the loggers
   ([6.4](6-4-equipment.md)).
4. **Act when a limit is passed**: stop the affected work, assess results
   produced meanwhile ([7.10](../process/7-10-nonconforming-work.md)), and
   record what you did.
5. **Control access** with locks, signs and a visitor log, as far as the
   work needs it.
6. **Plan site work.** Before testing at a customer's site, check the
   conditions there will allow a valid test, and record them.

## Worked example

A made-up biofuel lab has three rooms that matter:

| Room | Activity | Conditions that matter | Limits (from methods and manuals) | Monitoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Preparation | Milling, dividing | Dust; noise | Extraction on; separate from rooms 2 and 3 | Daily check of extraction |
| 2 Balance room | Weighing | Temperature, draughts, vibration | 18–25 °C; door closed while weighing; balance on stone table | Data logger, 15-minute readings |
| 3 Calorimetry | Calorific value | Stable temperature | 20–25 °C, no direct sun | Data logger, alarm |

**An event.** In July, the logger in room 3 records 27 °C for three hours
on a hot afternoon. The alarm emails the technical manager. Two calorific
value tests ran during that period. The lab:

1. Holds the two results.
2. Checks the calorimeter's benzoic acid check from that afternoon: within
   limits.
3. Repeats one of the tests the next morning: the difference is within the
   repeatability limit.
4. Releases both results, records the event and its assessment, and moves
   the calorimeter away from the window. Blinds are fitted.

## Common findings

- **Conditions specified but not monitored**, or monitored but nobody
  looks at the records.
- **Limits passed with no assessment** of the results affected.
- **Data loggers not calibrated.**
- **Incompatible activities side by side**: milling next to the balance,
  ovens next to the calorimeter.
- **Site work with no record** of the conditions on site.
