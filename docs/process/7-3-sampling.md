# 7.3 Sampling

## In one sentence

If your lab takes the samples it later tests, plan how you take them so they
represent what the customer cares about, follow that plan, and record what
you actually did.

!!! info "Does this clause apply to you?"
    It applies when your lab (or someone working for it) takes samples from
    a material, product or site for later testing or calibration. If
    customers bring samples to you, your results describe the sample as
    received, and you should say so on the report. Your sample handling is
    then covered by [7.4](7-4-item-handling.md).

## What an assessor looks for

- **A sampling plan and method** for each kind of sampling you do, based
  on a recognised standard where one exists, available at the sampling
  site.
- **Plans that address the factors that matter** for the result to be
  valid: how many increments, from where, how large, how combined, and how
  the sample is reduced, sealed and transported.
- **Sampling records** for every job: who sampled, when and where, which
  plan and equipment, the conditions (weather, temperature, state of the
  material), the location of each increment (a sketch, photo or GPS
  position), and any deviation from the plan.
- **Deviations agreed with the customer** and stated on the report.
- **Competent samplers** who were trained and authorised for each kind of
  sampling ([6.2](../resources/6-2-personnel.md)).
- **Sampling equipment** that is suitable, clean and, where relevant,
  checked or calibrated.

## How to do it in a small lab

1. **Start from the question.** Does the customer want to know about the
   whole delivery, one silo, or one bag? The answer sets what the sample has
   to represent.
2. **Use the sampling standard for your material.** Most fuel, food,
   environmental and construction materials have one. It tells you the
   minimum number and size of increments for a given lot size and particle
   size.
3. **Write a plan per job type** with:
    - the lot and what the sample represents;
    - the number, size and location of increments;
    - the equipment;
    - how increments are combined and divided;
    - containers, sealing, labelling and transport conditions.
4. **Record as you go**, on a form at the site, not from memory afterwards.
   A phone photo of the sampling point is cheap and convincing evidence.
5. **Record and report deviations.** If the plan could not be followed
   (a truck could only be reached from one side, the pile was frozen), say
   what you did instead, tell the customer, and mention it on the report.
6. **Remember uncertainty.** When you sample, the variation between
   increments is often bigger than the test's own uncertainty. If the
   customer's question is about the lot, sampling belongs in your
   uncertainty estimate ([7.6](7-6-measurement-uncertainty.md)).

## Worked example

A made-up biofuel lab samples a 25-tonne delivery of wood pellets for
moisture and ash, on behalf of the buyer.

| Plan item | What the lab's plan says |
|---|---|
| Lot | One truck delivery, 25 t, unloaded onto a conveyor |
| What the sample represents | The whole delivery |
| Method | The lab's procedure based on the solid biofuel sampling standard |
| Increments | Taken from the falling stream at the conveyor end, spread evenly over the unloading time. Number and volume worked out with the sampling standard's method for the required precision. For solid biofuels that is never fewer than 10 increments; very large lots are first split into sub-lots, each sampled on its own |
| Equipment | Stream-sampling cup, cleaned and dry |
| Combining and division | Increments combined into one bulk sample, reduced by riffle divider to two laboratory samples |
| Containers | Airtight bags, filled and sealed at once to stop moisture change; labelled with job number, date, time and sampler |
| Transport | Same day, kept away from heat |

**Record.** The sampler fills in a sampling record on site, logging the
start and end time of unloading, the time
of each increment, the weather (light rain; conveyor covered) and a photo of
the sampling point.

**Deviation.** The conveyor stopped twice, so two increments were taken a
few minutes late. The sampler notes it; the technical manager judges it has
no effect on representativeness and it is mentioned on the report.

**Report.** "Sampled by the laboratory according to plan SP-03. Results
apply to the delivery as sampled."

## Common findings

- **No written sampling plan**, or a plan that only names a standard
  without the job-specific details.
- **Sampling records completed afterwards**, or missing the location and
  conditions.
- **Deviations not recorded**, or recorded but not reported to the
  customer.
- **Samplers not authorised**, especially temporary or field staff.
- **Sample integrity lost** in transport: moisture samples in containers
  that are not airtight, or left in a hot vehicle.
- **Reports that do not say who sampled**, so the reader cannot tell whether
  results apply to the lot or only to the sample received.
