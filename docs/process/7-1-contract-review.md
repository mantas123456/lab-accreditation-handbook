# 7.1 Review of requests, tenders and contracts

## In one sentence

Before you accept work, make sure you and the customer agree on what will be
done and how, that you can actually do it, and keep a record of that
agreement and of any later change.

## What an assessor looks for

- **A defined review step** before work starts, for every kind of request:
  quotes, standing contracts, one-off phone orders and repeat work.
- **Records of the review**: who checked it, when, and what was agreed.
  For routine repeat work a simple record is enough; for new or unusual
  work it should show the checks were actually made.
- **Methods agreed with the customer.** If the customer asked for an
  unsuitable or out-of-date method, the lab told them.
- **Capability checked**: the method is in your scope (or the customer
  knows it is not), and you have the staff, equipment and time.
- **Subcontracted work identified** and agreed with the customer in
  advance.
- **The decision rule agreed** whenever the customer wants a statement of
  conformity, such as "pass" or "meets specification".
- **Changes handled properly**: an amendment after work started was
  reviewed again, agreed, and passed on to the people doing the work.

## How to do it in a small lab

1. **Use one checklist for every new request.** Keep it short enough that
   people actually use it. The questions that matter:
    - What exactly does the customer want measured, on what, and why?
    - Which method? Is it the current version? Is it in our scope?
    - Do we have the people, equipment, reference materials and capacity
      to meet the deadline?
    - Is any part subcontracted? Does the customer agree?
    - Does the customer want a pass/fail statement? Which specification,
      and which decision rule?
    - Any special requirements: sample amount, return of samples,
      confidentiality, report language, reporting uncertainty?
2. **Scale the effort.** A repeat order under an existing contract needs
   only a check that nothing has changed. A new type of test needs the
   full checklist and the technical manager's sign-off.
3. **Confirm in writing.** An email reply or a signed quote is enough. Spoken
   agreements over the phone need a note in the job record.
4. **Tell the customer about anything that is not normal.** Methods outside
   your scope, deviations from the standard method, or results that will not
   be covered by your accreditation mark.
5. **Treat changes as a new review.** If the customer changes the request
   after work starts, review it again and tell the analysts.

## Worked example

A boiler manufacturer asks a made-up biofuel test lab for an emissions test
on a new pellet boiler, plus moisture, ash and calorific value of the test
fuel, with the report in three weeks.

| Check | Finding | Action |
|---|---|---|
| What is wanted | CO, OGC, NO<sub>x</sub> and dust at nominal and part load; fuel analysis | Confirmed by email |
| Methods | Customer named an older edition of the boiler standard | Lab proposed the current edition; customer agreed |
| Scope | Emissions and fuel tests in scope; dust measurement not | Customer told that dust results will not carry the accreditation mark |
| Capacity | Test rig free in week 2; one authorised operator on leave | Booked with the second authorised operator |
| Subcontracting | Chlorine in the fuel goes to a partner lab | Customer agreed in writing |
| Conformity statement | Customer wants "meets class limits" on the report | Decision rule agreed: guarded acceptance with the lab's expanded uncertainty (see [7.8](7-8-reporting.md)) |
| Change | In week 2, customer adds a second fuel | Reviewed again; deadline moved by four days and agreed |

The checklist, emails and the amendment are filed in the job record.

## Common findings

- **No record of review** for orders taken by phone or for repeat customers.
- **Methods outside the scope** reported with the accreditation mark, or
  without telling the customer.
- **Subcontracting the customer did not know about.**
- **Pass/fail statements without an agreed decision rule.**
- **Changes not passed on**: the contract was amended but the analysts
  worked from the original request.
- **Out-of-date method editions** accepted without comment.
