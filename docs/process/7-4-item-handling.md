# 7.4 Handling of test and calibration items

## In one sentence

From the moment an item arrives until it is returned or disposed of, know
which item is which, keep it in a state that does not change the result,
and record anything unusual about it.

## What an assessor looks for

- **A procedure** for transport, receipt, handling, protection, storage,
  retention and disposal or return of items.
- **A unique identity for every item** that stays with it, and with every
  subsample, through the whole lab: on containers, worksheets, instrument
  files and the report.
- **Condition recorded at receipt**: anything that departs from normal or
  from what was agreed (damaged packaging, too little sample, wrong
  container, high temperature), and what was decided about it.
- **Customer consulted** when an item is not suitable, with the decision
  recorded; if testing goes ahead, a note on the report.
- **Storage conditions** that suit the items (temperature, humidity, light,
  segregation), monitored and recorded where they matter.
- **Retained samples** kept for a defined period, so results can be checked
  or repeated if questioned.

## How to do it in a small lab

1. **Log at the door.** Every item gets a lab number at receipt, before it
   is opened or split. Use the same number on every subsample, crucible,
   vial and data file.
2. **Check against the request.** Is this the item the contract
   ([7.1](7-1-contract-review.md)) described? Is there enough of it? Is the
   container suitable for the test?
3. **Record the condition** with a short, fixed list (packaging intact,
   seal intact, quantity sufficient, temperature on arrival if relevant)
   plus free text and a photo when something is wrong.
4. **Ask before you test a doubtful item.** Contact the customer, record
   what they decide, and carry a note to the report.
5. **Store to protect the result.** For moisture: airtight and cool. For
   volatile or biological tests: chilled, monitored, time-limited. Keep
   received, in-test and retained items apart.
6. **Set retention periods** by test type and contract, and dispose of
   items in a recorded way when the period ends, or return them if agreed.

## Worked example

A pellet sample for moisture, ash and calorific value arrives by courier at
a made-up biofuel lab.

**At receipt.** The lab assistant gives it number 26-0412 and checks it
against the order. The outer bag is torn along one seam. The inner bag is
intact, but the assistant cannot be sure it was sealed throughout
transport. The assistant takes a photo and records "outer bag torn; inner
bag intact; moisture result may not represent the sample as taken".

**Decision.** The lab emails the customer. Moisture is the property most
affected by a broken seal; ash and calorific value on a dry basis are
not. The customer asks the lab to test all three. The decision is recorded
in the job file.

**Testing and report.** Subsamples are labelled 26-0412-M, -A and -CV.
The report carries the note: "Sample received with damaged outer packaging.
At the customer's request, all tests were performed. The moisture result
may not represent the material at the time of sampling."

**Retention.** The rest of the sample is resealed, labelled and kept for
three months in the retained-sample store, then disposed of and logged.

## Common findings

- **Subsamples without the full lab number**, or numbers written by hand on
  crucibles that wear off.
- **Condition at receipt not recorded**, or only "OK" with no criteria
  behind it.
- **Unsuitable items tested without asking the customer** and without a
  note on the report.
- **Storage conditions not monitored** where they matter (fridges without
  temperature records).
- **No defined retention period**, or retained samples that cannot be found.
