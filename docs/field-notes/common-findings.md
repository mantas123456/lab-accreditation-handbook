# Ten common findings across the clauses

<p class="clause-badge clause-badge--draft">Field note · Draft</p>

*Draft: written, not yet reviewed by a second practitioner.*

Every chapter in this handbook ends with a list of common findings. Read
them side by side and the same few problems turn up again and again, only
dressed in the clothes of a different clause. This page collects the ten
that appear in the most chapters. It is built only from those lists; follow
the links for the detail.

The order is not a ranking. How often a finding is raised depends on the
lab, the assessor and the accreditation body. What these ten have in common
is that each one shows up under several clauses, so fixing the habit behind
it clears more than one finding.

## 1. Data collected but never looked at

Records exist, but nobody reviews them. QC charts with no record of a
trend review ([7.7](../process/7-7-validity-of-results.md)), environmental
logs nobody reads ([6.3](../resources/6-3-facilities.md)), calibration
certificates filed without being checked against acceptance criteria
([6.4](../resources/6-4-equipment.md)), customer feedback that is collected
but not analysed ([8.6](../management/8-6-improvement.md)).

**The habit to build:** every record that is meant to be checked has a
named person, a frequency, and a place to sign or note that it was checked.

## 2. Fixing the problem but not looking back

The oven, the balance or the control chart is put right, but nobody asks
which results were produced while it was wrong. This appears in
[6.4](../resources/6-4-equipment.md), [6.3](../resources/6-3-facilities.md),
[7.7](../process/7-7-validity-of-results.md) and
[7.10](../process/7-10-nonconforming-work.md).

**The habit:** after any failed check, write down the last good check, the
results since, and whether any of them need a retest or an amended report.

## 3. "Human error" as the root cause

Corrective actions that stop at "analyst retrained", or findings closed
with a correction only ([8.7](../management/8-7-corrective-action.md)).
Questionable PT results closed as "random error"
([7.7](../process/7-7-validity-of-results.md)). Complaints not linked to a
nonconformity ([7.9](../process/7-9-complaints.md)).

**The habit:** ask why the error was possible, and where else it could
happen. See [After the assessment](after-the-assessment.md) for a short
method.

## 4. Actions with no follow-up

Risks listed but never acted on ([4.1](../general/4-1-impartiality.md),
[8.5](../management/8-5-risks-and-opportunities.md)), audit findings with
no recorded follow-up ([8.8](../management/8-8-internal-audits.md)),
corrective actions open for months
([8.7](../management/8-7-corrective-action.md)), and actions from the last
management review never revisited
([8.9](../management/8-9-management-review.md)).

**The habit:** one action list for the whole lab, with an owner and a due
date on every line, reviewed at a fixed meeting.

## 5. Documents that do not match practice

A manual copied from another lab or a consultant
([8.2](../management/8-2-documentation.md)), a generic impartiality register
([4.1](../general/4-1-impartiality.md)), role descriptions that do not match
who actually does the work ([5](../structure/5-structure.md)), and an
Option A system that exists on paper only
([8.1](../management/8-1-options.md)).

**The habit:** write procedures from what the analysts actually do, then
improve them. An assessor tests the document against the bench.

## 6. Old editions and uncontrolled copies

Superseded editions of standards still in use
([8.3](../management/8-3-document-control.md),
[8.2](../management/8-2-documentation.md)), new editions adopted without
checking what changed ([7.2](../process/7-2-methods.md)) or accepted in a
contract without comment ([7.1](../process/7-1-contract-review.md)), and
personal printed copies with handwritten changes.

**The habit:** a master list that includes external documents, checked for
new editions at a fixed interval.

## 7. Authority that does not match the work

Training records without an authorisation, or people authorising reports
they are not authorised for ([6.2](../resources/6-2-personnel.md)); no
deputies, so the wrong person signs when the usual one is away
([5](../structure/5-structure.md)); temporary staff, students and field
samplers working without records ([6.2](../resources/6-2-personnel.md),
[7.3](../process/7-3-sampling.md)).

**The habit:** one matrix of who may do what, including deputies, kept up to
date when people join, leave or change roles.

## 8. Coverage gaps

Something is done for some methods but not all: uncertainty budgets
([7.6](../process/7-6-measurement-uncertainty.md)), proficiency testing
([7.7](../process/7-7-validity-of-results.md)), verification
([7.2](../process/7-2-methods.md)), or internal audits that skip clauses
and methods for years ([8.8](../management/8-8-internal-audits.md)).

**The habit:** a table with every method in scope as a row and every
requirement as a column. Empty cells are the work list.

## 9. Records that cannot show what happened

Corrections that hide the original
([7.5](../process/7-5-technical-records.md)), electronic records changed
without an audit trail, shared logins
([7.11](../process/7-11-data-management.md),
[4.2](../general/4-2-confidentiality.md)), and records altered without a
trace ([8.4](../management/8-4-records.md)).

**The habit:** strike through, never overwrite; one login per person; lock
spreadsheet formulas and record their version.

## 10. Records that cannot be found, or backups never tested

Records that take days to retrieve ([7.5](../process/7-5-technical-records.md),
[8.4](../management/8-4-records.md)), retained samples that cannot be found
([7.4](../process/7-4-item-handling.md)), and backups that nobody has ever
restored ([7.11](../process/7-11-data-management.md),
[8.4](../management/8-4-records.md)).

**The habit:** once a year, pick an old report and trace it back to its raw
data, equipment and staff, and restore one backup to a spare computer. Time
both.

## A made-up example

A small fuel-testing lab ran this list against its own system before its
first assessment. It found three of the ten in an afternoon: QC charts
plotted but never reviewed (1), two methods with no uncertainty budget (8),
and a shared login on the data system (9). None was hard to fix. All three
would very likely have become findings.
