# The accreditation journey

This page walks through what happens between "we want to be accredited" and
"we are accredited", and what happens afterwards. Every accreditation body
has its own rules, forms and timelines; read your body's documents before
you start.

## The steps

Prepare → apply → document review → on-site assessment → findings and
corrective actions → decision → surveillance → reassessment, and around
again.

1. **Prepare.** Build and run the management system, verify or validate
   the methods, estimate uncertainty, start QC and PT. Most bodies expect
   to see the system *working*, which means records from a few months of
   operation, at least one full internal audit and a management review.
2. **Apply.** Send the application with the proposed scope: which tests,
   on which materials, by which methods, over which ranges.
3. **Document review.** The body's assessors read your manual, procedures,
   methods and some records, and tell you about gaps before the visit.
4. **Pre-assessment (optional).** Some bodies offer a short visit to check
   readiness. It costs extra, but can save a failed assessment.
5. **On-site assessment.** A lead assessor checks the management system and
   one or more technical assessors check the methods. Expect them to:
    - watch analysts carry out tests;
    - trace old reports back through the records (a vertical audit);
    - ask analysts about uncertainty, QC rules and what they would do when
      a check fails;
    - check equipment, calibration certificates and traceability;
    - look at PT results and QC charts.
6. **Findings and corrective actions.** Findings are graded (the names vary
   by body). You get a deadline to send evidence that each one is
   corrected, with its root cause found and dealt with
   ([8.7](../management/8-7-corrective-action.md)).
7. **Decision.** A separate decision-maker at the body reviews the
   assessment and grants accreditation for the scope. You receive a
   certificate and a scope document. From then on you may use the
   accreditation mark, under the body's rules, on reports for tests in the
   scope.
8. **Surveillance.** Visits at intervals (often yearly) to check the system
   still works. They sample part of the scope each time.
9. **Reassessment.** A full assessment before the end of the accreditation
   cycle (several years; your body sets the length), and the cycle starts
   again.

Scope extensions (new tests) can be applied for at any time and are often
assessed during a surveillance visit.

can give the project real time. Larger scopes, or labs starting with no
validation data, need longer.

## What helps most

- **Measure where you stand.** Run the [gap analysis](../tools/gap-analysis.md)
  at the start and again every couple of months; the to-do list becomes your
  project plan.

- **Start QC and PT early.** You need months of data to set QC limits,
  estimate uncertainty and show the assessor your results are under
  control.
- **Involve the analysts from day one.** The assessor will talk to them,
  not only to the quality manager.
- **Keep the scope small at first.** Accredit the methods you run most,
  then extend.
- **Do a mock assessment** with someone from outside the lab before the
  real one. The [pre-assessment readiness checklist](../downloads/pre-assessment-checklist.docx)
  (Word) lists what the assessor will ask to see, clause by clause.
