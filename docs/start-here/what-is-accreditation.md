# What accreditation is

## In short

**Accreditation** is an independent, formal confirmation that a laboratory
is competent to carry out **specific** tests or calibrations, and that it
runs a system that keeps its results valid. For testing and calibration
labs, the requirements are set out in **ISO/IEC 17025**.

The key word is *specific*. A lab is never accredited "in general". It is
accredited for a **scope**: a list of tests or calibrations, materials,
methods and ranges. Anything outside that list is not covered.

## Who grants it

Accreditation is granted by an **accreditation body**. In the European
Union, each country has one national accreditation body, appointed under EU
rules. The bodies themselves are checked by their peers, and they work to
their own standard, ISO/IEC 17011.

National bodies that pass peer evaluation sign international agreements:
the **EA MLA** in Europe and the **ILAC MRA** worldwide. Because of these
agreements, a result from a lab accredited in one signatory country is
accepted in the others, without the customer having to check the lab
again.

## Accreditation is not certification

| | ISO 9001 certification | ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation |
|---|---|---|
| What it says | The organisation has a quality management system | The lab is technically competent for the tests in its scope |
| Who checks | A certification body | The national accreditation body, with technical experts in the field |
| What is assessed | Processes and documents | People, methods, equipment, traceability, uncertainty, QC, and the system around them |
| Covers results? | No | Yes, for tests in the scope |

A lab can hold both, but only accreditation tells a customer that its
*results* can be trusted.

## Why labs seek it

- **Regulators require it.** Many laws accept test results only from
  accredited labs, for example for emissions, food safety or product
  conformity.
- **Customers and tenders ask for it.** Large buyers and certification
  schemes often name "accredited to ISO/IEC 17025" as a condition.
- **Results are accepted abroad**, thanks to the international agreements.
- **It makes the lab better.** The discipline of uncertainty budgets, QC,
  PT and internal audits catches problems that would otherwise reach
  customers.

## What it costs

Accreditation takes time and money: preparing the system, PT schemes,
calibrations, the assessment fees, and staff time for audits and reviews.
Fees and timelines differ between accreditation bodies; check your
body's published fee schedule.
