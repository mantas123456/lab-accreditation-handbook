# 8.8 Internal audits

## In one sentence

Check your own lab at planned intervals, against the standard and your own
procedures, so you find problems before the assessor or a customer does,
and make sure the findings lead to action.

## What an assessor looks for

- **An audit programme** that covers every clause of the standard and every
  activity in your scope over a defined period, with frequency based on
  importance, changes and past results.
- **Criteria and scope** for each audit: what is being checked against what.
- **Objective auditors**: people who do not audit their own work, and who
  are trained and competent to audit.
- **Technical audits**, not only document checks: witnessing tests,
  following a sample from receipt to report.
- **Results reported** to the relevant management.
- **Correction and corrective action** without undue delay.
- **Records** of the programme, the audits and the follow-up.

## How to do it in a small lab

1. **Plan a programme** covering all clauses and all methods, usually over
   one year. Put more audits on areas with problems, changes or high risk.
2. **Mix three audit types:**
    - **Horizontal**: one clause across the whole lab (for example,
      document control everywhere).
    - **Vertical**: one report traced back through everything behind it:
      contract, receipt, test, records, equipment, QC, staff authorisation,
      review.
    - **Witness**: watching an analyst do a test and comparing with the
      SOP.
3. **Keep auditors independent.** In a lab of five, swap: the analyst
   audits the quality manager's area, the quality manager audits the
   technical work with help from someone competent in the method. An
   external auditor once a year can fill gaps.
4. **Use a short checklist** per audit, but follow the evidence.
5. **Write findings clearly**: what was found, which requirement, the
   evidence. Grade them (nonconformity, observation).
6. **Feed findings into corrective action**
   ([8.7](8-7-corrective-action.md)) and report a summary at management
   review.

## Worked example

A made-up biofuel lab's yearly audit programme:

| Month | Audit | Type | Auditor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feb | 4 Impartiality and confidentiality; 5 Structure | Horizontal | Technical manager |
| Mar | Moisture: report 26-0412 | Vertical | Quality manager |
| Apr | 6.4 Equipment, 6.5 Traceability | Horizontal | Senior analyst |
| May | Calorific value test | Witness | Quality manager + external expert |
| Jun | 7.1 Contract review, 7.8 Reporting | Horizontal | Senior analyst |
| Sep | Ash: report 26-0730 | Vertical | Quality manager |
| Oct | 8.2–8.9 Management system | Horizontal | External auditor |
| Nov | CHNS test | Witness | Technical manager |

**Vertical audit in March.** The quality manager follows report 26-0412
from the order to the report. Findings:

| # | Finding | Grade | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Report states dry basis; order asked for as received | Nonconformity | Corrective action CA-26-04 ([8.7](8-7-corrective-action.md)) |
| 2 | Oven thermometer certificate does not cover 105 °C | Nonconformity | Range extended at next calibration ([6.5](../resources/6-5-traceability.md)) |
| 3 | Training record for analyst E not signed by the trainee | Observation | Signed |

## Common findings

- **Audits that never cover the technical work**: only document checks.
- **Clauses or methods missing** from the programme for years.
- **Auditors auditing their own work.**
- **Findings with no follow-up**, or follow-up not recorded.
- **"No findings" audits year after year**, which suggests the audits are
  not looking.
