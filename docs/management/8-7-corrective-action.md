# 8.7 Corrective action

## In one sentence

When something goes wrong, fix the immediate problem and its consequences,
then find out why it happened, remove the cause so it does not happen
again, and check that your fix worked.

## What an assessor looks for

- **Reaction to the nonconformity**: the problem controlled and corrected,
  and its consequences dealt with.
- **A decision on whether to act on the cause**, by reviewing what
  happened, finding the cause, and asking whether similar problems exist
  or could happen elsewhere.
- **Root cause analysis** that goes beyond "human error".
- **Actions taken** in proportion to the effect of the problem.
- **A check of effectiveness** after a reasonable time.
- **Updates** to the risk register and the management system where needed.
- **Records**: the problem, the cause, the actions and the results.
- **Timeliness**: corrective actions closed, not open for months.

## Correction or corrective action?

| | Correction | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Fix this problem now | Stop it happening again |
| Example | Repeat the test; issue an amended report | Change the procedure; add a check; retrain |
| When | Always | When the cause could make the problem happen again |

## How to do it in a small lab

1. **Correct first**: contain the problem and deal with affected results
   ([7.10](../process/7-10-nonconforming-work.md)).
2. **Decide whether a corrective action is needed**, and record the
   decision. A one-off typing error caught at review may only need a
   correction; a repeated error does need corrective action.
3. **Find the root cause.** Ask "why?" until you reach something the lab can
   change (the five whys), or use a cause-and-effect diagram. Involve the
   people who do the work.
4. **Ask "where else?"** Could the same cause affect other methods,
   instruments or people?
5. **Choose actions that remove the cause**, assign an owner and a date.
6. **Check effectiveness** after enough time for the problem to show again,
   for example three months. Did it happen again?
7. **Update** risks, procedures and training as needed, and close the
   record.

## Worked example

An internal audit at a made-up biofuel lab finds that two of 20 reports
checked state results on a dry basis while the customer asked for "as
received".

**Correction.** Both customers are contacted and amended reports are issued
([7.8](../process/7-8-reporting.md)).

**Root cause (five whys):**

1. Why were the results on the wrong basis? *The analyst copied the dry
   basis values into the report.*
2. Why? *The report template has one result column, and the basis is typed
   in a note.*
3. Why did the review not catch it? *The reviewer checks numbers against
   the worksheet, not against the order.*
4. Why? *The review checklist does not include "basis as ordered".*
5. Why? *The checklist was written before the lab offered "as received"
   results.*

**Where else?** The same gap could affect calorific value (gross or net,
dry or as received). It does: one more case is found and corrected.

**Actions:**

| Action | Owner | Due |
|---|---|---|
| Report template: basis selected from a list, shown in every row | Quality manager | 1 month |
| Review checklist: add "basis and units as ordered" | Technical manager | 2 weeks |
| Contract review form: basis is a required field | Quality manager | 2 weeks |
| Brief all staff on the change | Technical manager | 1 month |

**Effectiveness.** Three months later the quality manager checks 30 reports:
all on the ordered basis. The action is closed and the risk register is
updated.

## Common findings

- **"Human error" or "retrained the analyst" as the cause and action**,
  with no look at why the error was possible.
- **No effectiveness check**, or one done the day the action closed.
- **Corrective actions open for months** with no follow-up.
- **"Where else?" never asked**, so the same problem appears in another
  method.
- **Accreditation body findings closed** with a correction only.
